There have been countless posts and research and endless online and offline debates on this topic over the last few years,  
but I've yet to see one that articulates exactly the problems I have with it.

So in this post I'll try to do it myself and elaborate how ai "blocks" your mind as a programmer or really any role that has a creative aspect, that encompasses a thought process. So even when I'm talking about programming, you can project it unto your domain.

The most naive way to put it is of course "it makes you think less", but that's not really it, it's missing something. Especially when the counterpoint is the classic "it allows you to think *more* about the *more* important stuff". In this post I want to try and articulate the actual problem.

## first, definitions

If we're talking about the loss of an ability, we first must clarify what precisely is the ability being discussed.  
How does a programmer that is capable and has the ability to do things, to build things, to fix things, gain _it_?

As I see it, by being familiar with the workings of sub-components, their interactions and behaviors, he is able to construct a mental model of the wider system and the domain on which it operates.

Next up is internalization, and you can look at it from different kinds of perspectives, so I'll list the only two I could come up with:  
- the programmer's perspective: by having a mental model of the problems, the mind spawns some background threads that are able to take on "mental mapping tasks" and draw conclusions on varying aspects of the problem domain.
that may be how sometimes ideas just pop into your head, how when not actively working on a task you get a lightbulb moment in the shower. 

- the theologian's perspective: it's the divine (citation needed) reaching out to you in a prophecy-like manner, but the only way it does that is if you make a sacrifice, the sacrifice being your mind. You give away your mind into the problem, in return, you gain insignts.

Whichever way you choose to look at it, you have to admire how absolutely incredible that ability is, and how incredibly disastrous it would be to lose it.

## vibe disenchant 

A lot of people nowadays say stuff like "I read 0 lines of the code and it works" and well that may be, and you know what, maybe the thing you built would not collapse under its own weight and things will work out.

But will the divine reach out to those who don't reach it first? Would they be deservant of it?

You had the initial idea, maybe you had a some inspirations along the way from your environment.

But your creation will be confined to the mental space from which you entered the prompt, by not letting the problem collide with the walls of your mind and stretching it out, you've doomed it to be less than or equal to the prompt that gave it away.

The sporadic thoughts of "oh I can actually do X", "Y is redundant!", "I think doing Z could take out those 2 problems at once!" will simply not come to you if you keep your mind at the surface level of the problem. Intuition and ability have a price, it costs giving away your mind to the problem.

## "llms can do that too so what"

Whether or not LLMs can be conscious or not is not the topic of the post but I will say 3 things on it:

1. I don't think you can have the creative sporadic thoughts without consciousness.
2. It seems to me that for example, bugs, problems that LLMs fix, while can be real and impactful, are, on the truthful scale of things, low hanging fruits, fixing them is good (and it's one of the reasons I'm not anti-ai), but it's not pushing a boundary. They are predictable by the prediction machine because they are predictable, they are within the bounds of what is already possible, they are not the unknown becoming known, they are the known staying that way.
3. if you seriously believe the fact that an entity, that has your similar conscious ability (if it had), should rid you of your burden to think, and makes your creative thoughts obsolete to the world, maybe you didn't deserve it to begin with. It's an incomprehensible argument to me.


## proof?
Set aside AI for a minute, think about how design and creation usually work.
We'll take an industry example but again you may abstract out the details and map unto your environment.

When planning a system you of course, first design it. Maybe you have architects and a bunch of people who aren't devs being involved. During the process there could be dozens and more of aspects and big problems taken into account and into the design.

But what happens when you start to build? Suddenly hundreds and more of tiny little problems and little design decisions pop up that affect the whole, massively.  
None of which even existed at the high level of the problem.

Predictably enough, your devs come across them, maybe they mention them and everyone's back to the design board, maybe they handle it themselves silently, but what's clear is that those are either handled *consciously*, or the thing eventually falls apart.

I don't want things to fall apart. I don't think it can be held together without conscious thought.

## bottom line
I think you have to be conscious. I think it's your obligation to use those wrinkles on your brain. I fear that the kind that trades off consciousness for (false) efficiency will go extinct. 

Sorry for the doomer post but I think it's important.

## appendix
There are additional ways in which I think ai and agentic developments are harmful, most notably flow state disturbance.
Which is, if we take the analogy previously mentioned further, not a disturbance to the background threads of your mind, but a disturbance to the main thread of your mind.
You simply cannot stay in flow state if you keep going back and forth with a chatbot.  
However I do think it's possible to gain the benefits of this without avoiding ai completely, but again you have to consciously structure your ai usage differently to not lose one or the other.
